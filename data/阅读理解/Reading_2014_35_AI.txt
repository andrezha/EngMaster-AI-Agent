TITLE: Saving the Sounds of the Past
As more people communicate using global languages like English, Spanish, and Mandarin, many local dialects are quickly vanishing. In fact, nearly half of the world's 7,000 languages are expected to disappear by the end of this century, according to international cultural organizations.
In an attempt to stop this loss, researchers from various groups --- including global geography and education societies --- have spent years recording endangered languages and the unique cultures they represent.
Dr. Aris, a researcher at a major university who specializes in the folk music and oral history of Southeast Asia, is continuing that work. His latest book, The Sounds of the Delta, grew out of his years spent living and working alongside traditional musicians in rural Vietnam.
Documenting these songs and stories is only the beginning for Dr. Aris, who plans to include oral traditions from other regions across Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia. He is not satisfied with simply recording these sounds before they are lost forever.
While working in an old library in London, Dr. Aris discovered a huge amount of valuable material --- including old photographs, film reels, and handwritten notes --- that had been ignored and were in danger of being ruined by time.
Now, through two non-profit projects he started --- the Digital Heritage Project and the Global Story Project --- Dr. Aris has launched a campaign to make these documents, found in archives around the world, available not just to researchers but to the younger people in the communities where the stories first began.
Thanks to modern digital technology and the internet, Dr. Aris believes that these endangered traditions can be preserved and reconnected with the people who still live in those cultures today.
