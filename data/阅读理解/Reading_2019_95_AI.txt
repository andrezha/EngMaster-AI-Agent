TITLE: Problem-Solving in Crows
Crows seem to have a talent for logic.
A team of scientists trained four crows to link 20 different shapes with various amounts of food as a reward. The researchers then tested how the birds combined these shapes to get the best reward.
A scientist named Dr. Helen Miller described the experiment: The crows were placed in a space with a touch-sensitive panel. On one side, a shape would appear, and on the other, two shapes were shown together. For example, a square might appear on the left, while a triangle and a star were on the right. If the crows picked the left side, they would get five pieces of food; if they chose the right side, they would get the total amount of both shapes—ten in this case.
After many tests, the scientists noticed that the crows chose the higher value more than half the time. This showed they were doing a basic calculation, not just remembering each shape.
When the team looked at the data more closely, they saw that the crows sometimes underestimated a total when the two choices were close. For instance, they might pick a single shape representing 12 instead of two shapes that added up to 13. This happened regularly: when adding two values, the crows always focused on the larger one and only added a bit of the smaller one to it.
“This shows that quantity is handled in a specific way in their minds,” Dr. Miller says. “But in this test, they are clearly focusing more on the bigger value than the smaller one.”
