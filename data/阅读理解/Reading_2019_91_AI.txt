TITLE: Student Innovation in Cities
Traffic congestion is a serious problem for city planners. Vehicles from all over the city crowd the streets of the business district, so planners spend hours trying to fix the flow each week. How is the City Council solving this major problem? They are turning to high school students. But not just any students. They rely on the City Solutions class, led by teachers like Robert Green at Westview High.
The program is designed to connect classrooms with urban engineers. For the past two years, Green’s students have been studying ways to reduce traffic in busy areas, and they think they are close to a solution. “We don’t give them easy tasks. They have to work just like real engineers,” says a project director.
“There are no traditional exams,” Green says. “There is no graded homework. The only grade is, ‘Are you reaching your goal?’ Basically, they have to produce a workable plan and then present it to the City Council. Engineers come and review it personally, and it’s a tough process. It’s a professional evaluation of their work.”
Green says the program helps students with college applications and life skills. “These kids are so focused on their work that I just watch. I don’t need to lecture.” And that traffic problem? Green says his students are communicating daily with engineers, preparing a plan to test in the city soon.
