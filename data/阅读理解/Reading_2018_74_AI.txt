TITLE: The Loss of Local Crafts
Traditional crafts have been appearing and disappearing for centuries, but recently, fewer are appearing and many more are vanishing. When the world was made of small, isolated villages, local groups developed their own unique ways of making goods, independent of each other. Some experts believe that centuries ago, when the population was small, there were perhaps 10,000 different local craft styles worldwide.
Soon after, as people moved to cities, their crafts became more standardized and fewer in number. In recent years, global trade, industrial production, and the growth of large international brands have all caused many local crafts to disappear. Modern factory products are increasingly taking over.
At present, there are about 5,000 recognized traditional crafts. Their distribution is very uneven. Generally, urban areas have few unique crafts, often produced by many people, while remote areas have many, often kept alive by only a few people. Europe has around 300 traditional crafts, the Americas about 800, and Asia and the Pacific perhaps 2,500. The median number of practitioners for these crafts is only about 4,000. This means half of the world's crafts are practiced by fewer people than that.
Already, over 500 of the total 5,000 crafts are close to disappearing, with only a few elderly masters left. For example, a specific type of weaving in a small village might have only three remaining masters. None of these seem to have a high chance of survival as younger generations turn to modern jobs.
