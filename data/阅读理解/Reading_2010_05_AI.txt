TITLE: Sparky the Service Pony
When I was seven, my family brought home a small pony named Sparky. My sister and I both loved Sparky and shared the chores. One of us would brush him, another would clean his stable, and then there were walks and training games. Sparky, in return, showed kindness to everyone. What touched me most was how he would stand quietly by whoever was feeling sad or sick. We always felt better when Sparky was near.
One day, while I was getting his water, he chewed up one of my brother’s leather gloves, which had to be thrown away. I knew my brother would be upset, and I had to show Sparky that he had made a mistake. When I looked at him and said, “No, Sparky,” he lowered his head to the ground and walked slowly to the back of the field. I saw a look of regret in his eyes.
Sparky turned out to be more than just a farm animal. He became a part of the local community. People would stop by our fence and ask to pet him. He was the most gentle creature. Many times, during local events, a small child would come over and pull on his mane. He never kicked or tried to run away. Instead, he would make a soft snorting sound. This sometimes worried people because they thought he was angry. In fact, he was just being friendly and loved the attention.
Now many years have passed since Sparky was sold to a specialized youth camp. I still miss the quiet mornings spent with him.
