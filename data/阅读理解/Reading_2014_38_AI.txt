TITLE: The Rise of International Tech Interns
One of the latest trends in the American workplace is the hiring of international interns from Asia. For example, a placement agency in New York has seen requests for interns from South Korea and Japan jump from almost zero to 3,500 since 2010. This is happening all over the country.
"I thought it would be beneficial for our team to understand East Asian business culture early on," says Robert Miller, a manager at a software company, speaking about his junior staff. "I want to give them the opportunity to work in a global market in the future." After only four months of working with a 24-year-old intern from Seoul, his team can already handle basic business interactions in Korean, the manager says.
Elena Ross, a native of China who runs a marketing firm in Chicago with her American business partner, had another reason for hiring interns from her home country. She didn't want her local staff to lose their connection to global trends. "Since I have a background in both cultures, I wanted the office to stay exposed to different perspectives and languages," she says.
"Working daily with a native speaker is far more effective than just taking a weekly training course," says Dr. Sarah Thompson, a professor of professional development. "But employers must realize that just one internship is unlikely to change the whole company. True cultural mastery requires long-term collaboration and continuous effort."
The popularity of international interns has grown because more American firms want their employees to be globally competitive. It is expected that the demand for these young professionals will continue to rise in the coming years.
