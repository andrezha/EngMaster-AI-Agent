TITLE: An Unexpected Meeting in Chicago
On one of her trips to Chicago for a tech conference, Elena Rossi decided to take two colleagues out to lunch. They sat down in a quiet downtown cafe, and within moments, another guest was coming toward their table.
“Excuse me, are you from Seattle?” the professional, silver-haired woman asked Rossi. “I’m from Seattle as well.”
Without waiting for an answer, the woman sat down with Rossi’s group. When her own friend arrived, she also pulled up a chair.
“They began sharing all the latest news from the Seattle tech scene,” Rossi said. “I wasn’t sure what my colleagues from New York were thinking.”
Finding a ride in the busy Chicago rain is nearly impossible. By the time the group finished, it was pouring outside. Rossi’s new friends immediately called a private car service to help them get a ride. Heading back to the conference center, her colleagues were surprised at how a simple lunch had turned into a mini-hometown meeting.
“My colleagues said: ‘Now we see why you say your city is so connected,’” Rossi added. “And I said: ‘Now you know. These are the interactions that give me my best ideas.’”
Sitting in a chair in her office, Rossi, a focused figure in a professional suit, seemed happy with this story.
“I don’t invent the problems in my research,” she said of her work in the last 15 years. “I don’t have to.”
Engineers, designers, managers, and people in casual hoodies, Rossi’s ideas come from mornings spent talking with old mentors, from walks through the busy streets of her native Seattle, from conversations overheard in local coffee shops. It bothers Rossi that, at 50, her hearing has slightly declined. Sometimes, sitting in a crowded room, she hears only a small part of a particularly fascinating story.
