TITLE: A Professional Bond
I first met Dr. Robert Miller in 1985, when Professor Hill, the head of our research lab, introduced us in Chicago. When the university didn’t want me for the senior project—they wanted someone as famous as Robert—he stood up for me. I don’t know how many people would have done that; most would have just followed the administration’s rules.
The friendship that grew out of that project and our second study three years later was based on the fact that despite our age difference, we both came from a background of traditional hard science. We respected our craft and were focused on exploring the complex problems we were trying to solve. Both of us had the qualities that are common in great scientists: we were curious, determined, and liked to challenge each other—but always with deep respect. Those qualities were also the center of our relationship outside the lab.
We shared the belief that if you are lucky enough to be successful, you should give something back—he through his science scholarships and his free classes for underprivileged students, and me through my youth science foundation. Robert and I didn’t see each other very often, but sharing that belief brought us together. We supported each other through funding and by attending each other’s lectures.
I last saw him a few months ago. He had been having health issues lately. He and I both knew what the situation was, and we didn’t need to talk about it. Ours was a relationship that didn’t need a lot of words.
