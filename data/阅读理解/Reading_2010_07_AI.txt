TITLE: The Rise of Urban Climbing
Thousands of young professionals living in the city center will celebrate their weekends by heading for indoor climbing gyms. Never mind that the city’s flat landscape seldom offers real mountains. It is easy enough for high-tech climbing walls to provide a challenge for urban residents. And the rapid growth of a health-seeking middle class has created this new craze.
Since the first professional gym opened ten years ago, the sport has seen an amazing increase. There are now more than twenty major gyms. Sports shops in the city have added professional climbing shoes and chalk bags to their collections. Mr. Zhang, a manager of a newly-opened gym, sees the growth of an industry that could soon lead enthusiasts to travel to the famous rock walls of Europe. In recent years, outdoor parks offering natural rock climbing have opened in the countryside. But many are in distant areas and cannot match the high-quality safety equipment and professional trainers of indoor gyms.
The climbing craze is partly a result of the recent increase in metro lines. This has made gyms in the suburbs reachable to ordinary people who don’t want to drive. According to Mr. Zhang, about 50% of the visitors to his gym come by subway. The rest are organized groups from schools or local companies.
The problem is making a profit. Starting a climbing gym requires a lot of money; renting large spaces from the government, building high walls, buying safety ropes, and ensuring there are enough staff to manage them.
The gym where Mr. Zhang works cost nearly $3m to set up. And as often happens in this market, when a good idea appears, many others rush in and price competition begins. Many gyms now offer very cheap training classes, even though most people are quite new to the sport.
