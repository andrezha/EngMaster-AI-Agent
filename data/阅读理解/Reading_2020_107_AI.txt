TITLE: Humans Adapting to a Changing World
Humans are constantly changing due to evolution. As researchers study our DNA, they find that we have adapted to new environments in just a few thousand years. For instance, some people in high mountain areas can breathe easily in thin air. In parts of Europe, groups that keep cows have developed the ability to drink milk as adults.
Recently, a report in a scientific journal described a new kind of adaptation—this time to the sea. A group of people in Southeast Asia, known as the Sama-Bajau, have become expert divers. They live in coastal areas of the Philippines and Malaysia. Traditionally, they stayed on boats, but now many build houses on wooden supports over the water. “They are very different from people who live on land,” said a researcher who has studied them for years.
The researcher first met them when he was a child. They made their living by diving for fish or pearls. “We were very surprised that they could stay under the water much longer than anyone else,” he said. “They seemed very comfortable moving deep below the surface.”
In 2015, a genetics student heard about these people. She wondered if many years of diving had changed their bodies to make the work easier. “It looked like a perfect example of how people change to fit their environment,” she said. She also mentioned that there are likely specific genes that help them stay underwater.
