TITLE: The Green Yard Project
Since 2015, Westside City has been improving its urban environment. One by one, abandoned industrial sites are being changed into "Pocket Parks." So far, more than 120 small gardens have been created. Westside now has more public green spaces than any other nearby city.
The lots that were once ugly with trash are now covered with beautiful flowers and small paths, thanks to the Urban Green Program (UGP). Its work makes the streets and neighborhoods attractive, and its residents very happy. The program began as part of the City’s Clean Air Initiative. Jane Smith is the UGP’s director. “When people ask me what our program is about,” she says, “I answer them with one word: fresh.” Each year, the UGP offers community planting days and gardening workshops. Some local volunteers even help maintain the UGP parks.
The UGP’s work, says Smith, is all about developing a sense of community. When a neighborhood requests a park, the UGP works with the people there to decide on a theme. Some themes have been “Active Living,” “Nature for All,” and “Quiet Corner.”
The UGP receives up to 15 requests for new parks each week. Last year, the workers completed 20 gardens.
“The creation of a park enters people’s shared memory as a special, pleasant moment in neighborhood history,” says Smith, who began as a landscape designer in Seattle.
