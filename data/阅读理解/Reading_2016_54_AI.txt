TITLE: Sharing Skills through SkillSwap
Skill-sharing can be a community activity. Think of local community centers where people gather to learn cooking or painting. Now, the platform SkillSwap.com changes the traditional way we think about learning.
Users go to the site and list the specific skills they have and are willing to teach. SkillSwap provides a digital badge to display on their profile. Then the person offers a free introductory session in a public park or cafe, hoping that their knowledge will spread, reaching more people as each new learner passes the skill to someone else.
Jason Miller, the founder of SkillSwap, says, “The two things that truly change your path are the mentors you find and the skills you master. SkillSwap brings them together.”
Users hold sessions in libraries, coffee shops, and even public squares. Whoever joins a session will go to the site to confirm what they learned. People who attend can also write a review describing how the session helped them. Notifications are then sent to the mentors to keep them informed about how their knowledge is spreading. Jason Miller says the goal is to prevent people from being greedy with their expertise, letting it sit unused in their minds.
SkillSwap is part of a movement of people who want to return to "real-world" connections and not just online tutorials. The site now has over two million users in more than one hundred countries.
