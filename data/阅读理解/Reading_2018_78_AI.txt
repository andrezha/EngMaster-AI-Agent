TITLE: The Decline of Active Play
Children and teenagers are spending much less time on physical activity for fun, according to a Youth Health Report released this week.
While the drop over the last ten years is large for teenagers, some information in the report shows that active play is still important for many kids, and shows how parents can help encourage more movement.
According to the report, "the percentage of those who say they 'almost never' exercise for fun has gone from 10 percent of 12-year-olds in 1990 to 25 percent today."
The data shows that active play levels for younger children, ages 3-7, stay mostly the same. However, the amount of time spent in each play session has dropped, from about an hour to only thirty minutes.
When it comes to technology, the report points out that many parents still limit screen time, mostly because they are worried about its effect on their children's physical health and eyesight.
The most positive data shows that parents serve as important examples for their children when it comes to being active. Data shows that kids who exercise frequently have parents who are active themselves, have more sports equipment at home, and have parents who set aside time for family activities.
As the holidays approach, parents might take this chance to plan a family trip to a park or a sports center to encourage a more active lifestyle.
