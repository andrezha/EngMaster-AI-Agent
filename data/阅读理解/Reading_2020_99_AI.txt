TITLE: Smart Fabrics and the Future
The link between humans and technology has long been a topic of research. Recent studies have found helpful effects. For example, a study in a major city found that smart offices with better lighting and airflow saw an increase in worker health. In another, employees were found to be 20% more creative when their desks were equipped with smart organizers.
Engineers at a top institute have gone further, changing the materials in fabrics so they can perform unusual functions. These include clothes with sensors that show when the wearer is tired, and a fabric that can detect harmful pollutants in the air. “We are thinking about how we can design fabrics to replace the gadgets we use every day,” explained a professor of engineering.
One of his recent projects involves making fabrics glow using safe chemical reactions. The team found they could create a soft light for several hours. This light, though faint, is just the beginning. The technology could one day be used to light up rooms or even turn curtains into self-powered lamps.
In the future, the team hopes to create a version of the technology that lasts for the entire life of the fabric. They are also working on a "switch" that stops the glow when it is not needed.
Traditional lighting uses a lot of energy. Since power is often lost when it travels from a power station to distant street lights, much energy is wasted. Glowing fabrics could bring light directly to where it is needed and help save energy.
