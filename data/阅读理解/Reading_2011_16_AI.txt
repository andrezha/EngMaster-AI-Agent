TITLE: The History of Public Notices
The term "public notice" refers to any official announcement that brings information to the attention of the community. Throughout history, notices have been an effective way to share news and rules. In the Middle Ages, leaders used "heralds" to announce messages orally in town squares to inform the public. When printing was developed in the fifteenth century, notice sheets could be produced easily and were either posted on church doors or placed in town halls.
By the end of the seventeenth century, when journals were beginning to be read by more people, printed notices became an important way to share community updates. The City Journal was the first to set aside a section just for public announcements. This was so successful that by the end of the century, several offices started dedicated systems for making official notices for the public.
Notices spread quickly throughout the eighteenth century. Writers were starting to pay more attention to the decorative style of the text. Everything, from health rules to event dates, was shared with clear methods such as bold lettering, organized patterns, and the use of simple drawings and phrases easy to understand.
Near the end of the nineteenth century, organizations that were focused on the distribution of information came to be known as "information bureaus." The bureaus developed new ways to make people feel like informed citizens. Throughout the twentieth century, these bureaus promoted public awareness as a way of life, spreading the belief that a society could be healthy only if people were well-informed.
