TITLE: The Confidence Project
Today is presentation day for the junior interns at TechStart, and now it is Kevin’s turn. The 19-year-old is the most energetic member of the team. With his bright smile, he seems like someone who would thrive during a public pitch.
But he is visibly anxious. “Today I want to explain why we should... should...” Kevin gets stuck on a difficult technical term, a common hurdle for those new to the industry. His manager, Sarah, stands nearby, offering a quiet nod of encouragement. “...Adopt... this... plan.” Despite a few hesitations, Kevin delivers his points remarkably well. When he finishes, Sarah asks the rest of the team to share what they liked about his ideas.
The son of a local baker, Kevin joined the firm only four months ago. Sarah remembers how, at the start of the internship, when asked to present his updates, Kevin would often find an excuse to leave the room.
Learning professional communication can be a stressful journey. What you need is a supportive environment that allows for mistakes. “It takes a lot for any intern,” Sarah explains, “especially for someone finding their voice in a new field, to feel bold enough to say, ‘I am not sure, but I want to learn.’”
Sarah came up with this "Pitch Your Vision" project when she noticed that many interns didn't believe their ideas were valuable enough for a real company. The project is about more than just public speaking. She wants these young people to learn to recognize their own value.
“Promoting your own strengths,” Sarah says, “is very difficult for someone who enters a professional space without much confidence.”
