TITLE: Focus and the Power of Choice
A typical dog trainer in many people’s minds is someone using a loud whistle and a bag of treats. The whistle gets most of the attention, but it’s often the hand signals that do the most important work. When a trainer holds up several fingers or moves their hand in a complex way, the dog tries to focus on all parts of the movement at once. With its attention divided, the dog becomes still and waits for a clearer signal. When faced with multiple possible commands, the dog chooses to wait rather than act incorrectly.
How often do you find yourself in the same position as that dog? How often do you have a goal you want to reach --- like learning a new language, starting a project, or getting fit --- only to end up frozen by all the different methods and options in front of you?
This is a common problem because while "experts" are busy arguing about which specific plan is the best, people who just want to improve their lives are left confused by all the conflicting advice. The result is that we feel we can’t focus, or we focus on too many things at once. We take less action, make less progress, and stay where we are instead of moving forward.
It doesn't have to be that way. Whenever you feel like the world is giving you too many choices at once, remember this: you only need to focus on one thing. You just need to get started. Taking the first step before you feel "perfectly ready" is a common habit of successful people. If you have a dream to chase, a task to complete, or a person you want to become... take action immediately. If you are clear about your first step, the rest of the world will either help you or get out of your way.
