TITLE: Rolling through the Fuel Crisis
Cassandra Lee finds it hard to manage on her current budget. So this year she did something more than just for fun: She started cycling to work. For her first month, Lee has logged 200 miles on her bike. The family’s old garage has become a repair shop, with more tools arriving next month. Last year, Rita Green used her bike occasionally. This year she has made it her main transport because, she said, “The price of gas is going up and I was looking to get fit too; so it’s a win-win situation all around.”
They are among the growing number of people who, driven by higher fuel costs and a changing economy, have taken up cycling for the first time. Others have increased their daily riding distances. Bike shops and outdoor stores say not since the 1970s has there been such an increase in interest in commuting by bike. Now many bike models across the city have been sold out for several weeks. In some areas, specialized road bikes have a two-month waiting list.
George Smith, owner of a cycling company, said sales of commuter bikes and gear are up by 50% over last year, double the average growth of the last three years. Mr. Smith argues that some of the reasons have been building for a while. The big one is the sharp rise in the cost of fuel, together with the increases in the price of public transport. Travel costs have increased because of global market changes. People are driving less and taking fewer long trips, so there is more time and reason to cycle.
