TITLE: Eco-Friendly Fashion from Invasive Plants
When looking for an eco-gift for a friend, paper made from wild weeds probably isn't the first thing you'd think of. However, some environmentalists and paper designers are trying to create a market for stationery made from an invasive plant called water hyacinth.
Special art shows in Seattle and Portland have displayed paper products made from these plants in various forms. “It may sound strange to talk about 'green paper' from weeds—unless you realize that water hyacinths are choking local rivers every year,” says Mark Evans, a director at GreenWay.
Local environmentalists were so worried that they started a program to pay residents for clearing the plants. Some of these fibers are eventually used in the art shows like the one in Portland last month.
Water hyacinths were originally brought from South America for garden ponds but escaped into the wild. “The local ecosystem cannot survive this foreign species. It’s killing native fish. It’s a choice between the weeds and our water,” says Michael Reed, a local biologist.
The paper trade kept the weed population under control for years, but when the demand for handmade paper collapsed in the late 1990s, the plants spread rapidly.
Biologist Elena Costa runs a control program in the area. She says it’s hard to convince people that weed-based paper is high-quality, but she is certain of its value. Teams collect tons of plants a year, and part of Costa’s work is to encourage the use of this fiber.
Designer Sarah Jenkins admits it took her some time to believe that using invasive weeds for her art was a good idea. She is now working on a special mark to show that her paper products are truly helpful to the environment.
