TITLE: The Benefits of Focused Reading
Focused reading shares many mental benefits with deep study, while likely causing less mental fatigue. It does, however, have its own requirements.
Focused readers are disciplined individuals. The most demanding task in a literature course is a long-form analysis, which requires more concentration than a short summary. But the rules of deep reading require that a reader's mind stays fixed on the text and avoids all distractions. It is this specific form that makes focused reading such a valuable activity, says Dr. Jane Miller, a professor of cognitive science.
Like writing, focused reading is mentally demanding. According to calculations, a person reading deeply for an hour would process information much more effectively than someone skimming, although it takes more effort than casual browsing.
However, focused reading does not drain the mind as much as multitasking does, Dr. Miller says. According to her research, multitaskers switch focus frequently, which uses a lot of mental energy, while focused readers, who stay on one task, use their energy more efficiently.
As a result, some of the stress associated with multitasking, such as "information overload," is uncommon among focused readers. But the practice does require a lot of patience, so people who are used to quick updates might find it difficult at first. In fact, anyone wishing to try focused reading should probably start with short periods and gradually increase their time. It takes practice.
