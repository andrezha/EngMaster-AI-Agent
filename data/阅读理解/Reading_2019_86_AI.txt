TITLE: Behavioral Security
As digital fraud becomes more frequent, the demand for biometric security—like facial recognition—is rising to protect private data. However, these systems are often quite expensive to implement.
Researchers at a tech institute say they have created an affordable alternative: a smart mouse. This device monitors the exact speed at which a user moves the cursor and the pressure applied during clicks. The mouse provides an extra layer of protection by studying the user's movement patterns and the timing of their clicks. These behaviors are unique to each individual. Therefore, the system can verify a person's identity and decide whether to grant access to the computer, even if someone else knows the correct password.
The invention does not require users to learn any new skills. Almost everyone knows how to use a mouse, but everyone handles it differently.
In a test of this technology, researchers asked 100 participants to click a specific icon four times. The data gathered was used to identify different users based on their movement habits, with a very high success rate. The researchers believe the device is easy to produce on a large scale because it is made of low-cost, durable materials. The team plans to bring it to the market soon.
