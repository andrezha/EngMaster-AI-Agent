TITLE: Soft Skills Development in Campus Volunteering
While some parents believe expensive coding classes are the only way to prepare children for the future, researchers suggest that simple campus volunteering helps students develop vital interpersonal skills.
Professor Susan Miller, an expert on social development at State University, found that students who participate in community service between the ages of 12 and 15 later show much better emotional intelligence. Participation in such programs was found to be a key indicator of leadership potential after accounting for differences in family income, school quality and previous academic records, Miller said.
The research team observed 53 student groups during various extracurricular tasks and found that those involved in volunteering for 20 to 40 hours during early middle school performed significantly better in team cooperation when tested two years later.
“The students who engaged in volunteering activities showed a higher ability to manage conflicts and understand others’ perspectives compared to those who did not,” Miller stated.
The study asked teachers to let students interact naturally. About half of the students chose to volunteer at some point. It was noted that students from higher-income families tended to participate in service more often. While both boys and girls benefited from the experience, boys often took on more complex logistical tasks, and their mentors provided more technical feedback and were more involved in the process than they were with female students.
The study was recently shared in the Journal of Educational Psychology.
