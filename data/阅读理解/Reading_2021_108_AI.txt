TITLE: Iconic Libraries of the World
People have been gathering in grand libraries since ancient times. Around 300 B.C., the Library of Alexandria was built, which remains the most famous historical library and influenced later designs. While it was huge for its time, it was small compared to modern national libraries that can hold millions of volumes.
Today, modern standards—such as digital access and comfortable study spaces—keep library designs focused on the visitor's experience. Gone are the days of dark, dusty corners; most modern libraries are bright and open.
For our list of the world's most impressive libraries, we used data from international cultural organizations, ranking them by their book collections and architectural importance.
All these libraries are still active, open to the public, and host significant cultural events.
·British Library, London, UK. Collection: 170 million items. Opened: July 1, 1973.
·Library of Congress, Washington D.C., U.S. Collection: 167 million items. Opened: April 24, 1800.
·New York Public Library, New York, U.S. Collection: 55 million items. Opened: May 23, 1911.
·National Library of China, Beijing, China. Collection: 37 million items. Opened: April 24, 1909.
·Sainte-Geneviève Library, Paris, France. Collection: 2 million items. Opened: February 4, 1851.
