TITLE: The Courage to Innovate
“I didn’t hear my project called,” explained David Miller to Youth Tech (YT) as he talked about that thrilling moment on national TV when he won the "Young Inventor of the Year" award. “Are you ready?” is what he heard. Then he said, “I shook my head, and then they pointed to me and it was announced again.”
It was three days after that life-changing moment for the eighteen-year-old student from Denver—he was still on cloud nine.
“I was so surprised! I never believed my design could actually win.” Present in the room that day were his parents, his sister, his friends, and his science mentor.
Understanding why his family was there, YT asked why his mentor had traveled so far to see him. “He’s always been my role model. I’ve worked with him since I was ten. He’s faced so many technical failures and overcome them all. I’ve learned to deal with setbacks and move on because of him.”
One of those setbacks for David happened two years ago when a fire destroyed his workshop. He lost all his prototypes. He found that keeping a journal helped him get through the dark days. He said, “I write about my ideas. I write a lot, a lot, a lot...”
As the state winner, he traveled around giving talks to students about the importance of persistence. In his new role, David will continue to speak to youth about innovation safety and helping others through technology.
When YT asked the winner if he had any advice, he said, “Don’t let anyone change your vision. Stay with people who support your goals. That way, it is easy to stay true to yourself.”
