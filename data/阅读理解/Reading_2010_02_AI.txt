TITLE: The Unique Desert Lizard
Along the dry dunes of the Sahara, there lives a lizard that moves through sand as if it were swimming, runs like a desert wind, eats tough insects, and has sharp spikes on its tail when young. They build their burrows about 2 meters beneath the surface, a key feature for staying cool. It is known as the sand-diver.
In look, both male and female lizards appear quite similar with yellow-brown scales on the back and pale white on the belly. The head is flat, with large ears, dark eyes, and thick skin. Its most striking feature, though, is only found in the young.
Baby sand-divers have a row of sharp spikes along the tail. Using these spikes, together with their strong legs, they can dig quickly into the sand, looking much like ancient desert creatures must have done. When the young lizards grow older and their skin becomes tougher, they lose these spikes.
During the hot months between June and August, sand-divers move about the desert in groups of 15 to 25. But in September, when the cooler season begins, they collect together in smaller units of two to five for nesting purposes.
