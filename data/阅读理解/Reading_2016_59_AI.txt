TITLE: The New Rules of Information Sharing
Positive news travels. If it inspires, it stays. Negative news might grab attention, but positive news builds connection. Those are the new rules emerging for digital communication. Now that information is being shared and tracked in real-time, researchers are discovering that the old ideas are changing. By studying people’s social media posts and shares, scientists have found that helpful and happy news can spread faster than sad or scary stories.
“The ‘shocking’ rule works for traditional media,” says Professor Alan Smith, a researcher at the University of Michigan. “They want your clicks and don’t care how you feel afterward. But when you share a post with your community, you care a lot about their response. You don’t want to be seen as a source of constant negativity.”
Researchers studying word-of-mouth — instant messages, online reviews, and workplace talks — found that these interactions were more likely to be positive than negative. But this didn’t necessarily mean people only liked happy news. Was positive news shared more often just because life is generally good? To test this, Dr. Smith looked at how people shared a specific group of articles from a major news site. He analyzed the “most-shared” list for several months. One of his key findings was that articles about personal growth and new technology were much more likely to be shared than general news. He found that these topics amazed readers and made them want to share that feeling of progress with others.
Users also tended to share articles that were exciting or helpful, or that caused strong reactions like anger, but rarely articles that were just depressing. They wanted to feel some level of energy, and they preferred positive energy to negative. The more inspiring an article, the more likely it was to be shared.
