TITLE: Tech Families Reconnect
As a lifelong resident of Seattle, Elena Rossi never intended to leave her home. Even when her daughter and son-in-law asked her to move to San Francisco to help with their young children, she politely declined. Only after months of thoughtful conversation did Ms. Rossi finally agree. That was three years ago. Today, the whole family views the move as a success, as it has built a stronger bond than they would have had while living in different states.
No official data shows the exact number of grandparents like Rossi who are relocating to be near their adult children. However, evidence suggests that the trend is increasing. Even famous business leaders have seen their parents move closer to help with family duties. According to a survey by FamilyLink.com, 80 percent of people said such high-profile decisions will influence how other families think about their living arrangements. Two-thirds believe more families will follow this path.
“In the 1990s, we were all very focused on independence and couldn't move away fast enough to prove we could succeed on our own,” says Dr. Julian Vance, editor of Family First magazine. “We now realize how vital family support is and how important it is to be physically near them, especially when you are balancing a career and kids.”
Relocating is not for everyone. Almost every grandparent wants to be with their grandkids and is willing to help, but sometimes it is better to stay put and visit often. Living far away is difficult, especially knowing your children are busy and stressed, but leaving behind your own social circle and familiar life may be even harder.
