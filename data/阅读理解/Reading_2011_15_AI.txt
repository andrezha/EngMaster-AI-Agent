TITLE: The Bookstore Owner’s Gift
When I was a teenager growing up in a small town in the 1990s, we had a local bookstore owner who suggested books for our family. His name was Mr. Harris. He always wore a blue vest and worked in a shop filled with the smell of old paper. As a fourteen-year-old boy, I couldn’t take my eyes off the unique wooden bookmarks he kept on his desk. He noticed this one day and gave me a beautifully carved one for free.
Of course, he provided more than just books. There were maps, journals, and local newspapers. If we wanted a special book, my father would send a note—“Please find a copy of the new travel guide”—and leave it in the shop’s mailbox. And then, the book would magically appear on our porch.
All of this was about more than convenience. There existed a deep trust between families and Mr. Harris. He even had a key to our back door, so he could leave deliveries inside when it rained. I remember Mr. Harris from time to time taking a break in our living room, having a glass of lemonade and sharing news about the town.
There is sadly no local bookstore delivery today. Online giants allowed the sale of cheaper digital books, making it difficult for small shops to survive. Besides, books are available instantly on screens, and it may just not have been practical to have a personal service.
Recently, an old wooden bookmark I found in an attic brought back my childhood memories. I took it out and placed it on my current desk. Every so often my colleagues will ask where it came from. So I start sharing stories of my youth, and of the man who brought us wisdom along with his books.
