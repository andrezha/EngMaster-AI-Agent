TITLE: Clever Crows and Their Problem-Solving Skills
New Caledonian crows, a kind of bird known for their intelligence, have been shown to have problem-solving abilities similar to a young child. Although they use tools in the wild, they have proved even more skillful in controlled experiments. In a recent test, crows were shown a tube with a small piece of food at the bottom. The birds were given several wires of different lengths and shapes. By choosing the right wire and bending it into a hook, they could get the food.
In humans, children can use simple tools from a young age, but it takes more time for them to understand how to change a tool to solve a specific problem. This ability is a sign of high intelligence. In the experiment, most of the crows were able to pick the best wire and change its shape just by looking at the tube. When they made a mistake, they quickly tried a different way and succeeded. This shows that these crows have a strong understanding of how objects work in space.
The next step for researchers is to find out if the crows are born with this ability or if they learn it by watching other crows.
