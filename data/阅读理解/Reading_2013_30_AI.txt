TITLE: The Social Lies of "Movie Buffs"
It happened to me recently. I was telling a colleague how much I had enjoyed watching a classic documentary and how it had changed my opinion of the director. A friend I was with agreed immediately, calling it "a masterfully filmed work." However, he then started talking about the director in a way that made it obvious he knew nothing about the director’s actual career. I realized I was talking to a "movie liar."
And it seems my friend is not alone. Research suggests that nearly two-thirds of people have lied about watching a famous film that they haven't actually seen. In a recent "Cinema Secrets Report," that documentary I mentioned was among the top ten most lied-about films. The report lists various movies and directors that people frequently pretend to know. Since I don't like to lie (I’d be embarrassed if I were caught), I’ll admit now that I haven't watched every "must-see" film on that list. But I am happy to say that, unlike 40 percent of people, I have actually watched the number-one film on the list, Citizen Kane. I think it’s truly a masterpiece.
The report also found other interesting trends. It says many people lie about having seen films by directors like Alfred Hitchcock or Akira Kurosawa. When asked why they lied, the most common answer was to "look smart" or impress the person they were talking to. This can be dangerous if the conversation gets more detailed!
However, when asked which movies they truly enjoy, people named popular blockbusters. About 40 percent of those asked also admitted they often look up the ending of a movie online before they finish watching it. (I’ll be honest: I do this too, and I’m surprised that 60 percent claimed they never have.)
