TITLE: Digital Technology in Modern Cinema
When the latest ocean adventure film was released last week, a group of marine lovers gathered near the theater. But they weren't there to protest the use of plastic. Instead, one member, holding a large picture of a dolphin, had come with a message for the director: “Thank you for using digital sea life!”
The film’s production team used advanced computer technology to create realistic sea animals, spending millions on systems that capture the movements of actors and turn them into digital images. In this movie, the result was a group of very lifelike dolphins and whales.
However, this film is quite different from most others. In fact, many studios still use real animals for filming. A professional group, which checks how animals are treated in the movie industry, is currently following more than 1,500 projects this year. Several recent movies have already faced criticism from activists who believe the animals on set were not cared for properly.
Sometimes, the main concern isn't just what happens during filming; it’s the training methods and the small living spaces for these animals when they are not working. There are also worries about films produced in other countries, where the rules for animal protection are not as strict as those in the local industry.
