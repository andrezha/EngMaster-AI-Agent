TITLE: The Return of the Wild Cats
After much discussion, mountain lions were recently returned to the Highland Nature Reserve. Twelve animals were brought from a northern forest and released into the park. By last year, the mountain lion population had increased to over 80.
Mountain lions were once common throughout the Highland area and much of the region, but they were slowly pushed out by human expansion. By the early 1940s, these cats had almost disappeared from the reserve. They moved deeper into the remote mountains where there were fewer people.
The absence of the mountain lions led to many surprising changes. The number of deer—the lions’ main prey—increased very quickly. These deer ate huge amounts of grass and plants, which harmed the variety of plant life in the park. Without the lions, the numbers of small mountain dogs also grew. These dogs killed many of the park’s rare rabbits and forced away the local foxes.
As early as 1980, scientists asked the government to bring mountain lions back to Highland. They hoped the lions could help control the deer and small dog populations. Many local residents were against the plan because they were afraid the lions would harm their farm animals.
The government spent many years creating a plan to return the lions. Wildlife experts now carefully watch and manage the mountain lion groups. Today, people still talk about how well the lions are doing in Highland. Deer and small dog numbers have gone down, while rabbits and foxes have returned. The Highland project has been a helpful test to help scientists decide if lions should be returned to other areas as well.
