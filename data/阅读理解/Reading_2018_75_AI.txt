TITLE: Old Appliances and Energy Use
We may think we are a society that replaces old appliances as soon as a newer model comes out, but a study shows that we keep using our old machines long after they are out of date. This is bad for the environment and our bank accounts, as these old appliances use much more power than new ones that perform the same tasks.
To find out how much electricity these devices use, Sarah Jenkins and her team at the Energy Institute tracked the costs of each product through its life—from when it is manufactured to when it is no longer used. This provided a clear picture of how home energy use has changed since the 1990s. Devices were grouped by age—older refrigerators, basic washing machines, and old-style heaters.
Newer, smart appliances arrived in the early 2000s. However, as we bought more efficient ones, we didn’t always get rid of the old ones. For example, an old fridge might be moved to the garage to store drinks, and suddenly, you have two fridges running instead of one. The average number of large appliances rose from three per home in 1995 to seven in 2015.
According to Jenkins’ team, old heating systems and cooling units are the worst. Their energy use and contribution to carbon emissions more than doubled during the study period.
What is the answer? The researchers explored what would happen if people replaced old products with new ones that have multiple functions, such as a smart eco-unit that handles both heating and water. They found that using these modern, multi-use devices instead of several old ones could cut energy use by nearly 50%.
