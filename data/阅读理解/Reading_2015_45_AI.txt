TITLE: Optimize Your Workspace for Focus
Your office layout may have a significant effect on your productivity. Experts suggest that the way you organize your workspace could determine whether you stay focused or get distracted easily. You can make your environment work for your goals rather than against them. Here are some ways to turn your desk area into a focus-boosting zone.
Let in the natural light. Dimly lit environments are more likely to lead to tiredness, as people often feel less energetic when they are in dark spaces—and thus more likely to lose concentration. If your office lacks windows, add more desk lamps to fill the area with brightness.
Consider the colors. Research shows that bright, aggressive colors may cause mental fatigue. In one study, employees who worked in a soft green room were 25 percent more focused than those in a bright red or orange room. Calm colors like green or light blue help the brain stay steady, while "hot" colors can make us feel rushed. So when choosing office decor, go for green.
Watch the sound. People who work in quiet environments tend to complete tasks about 20 percent faster than those who deal with constant noise. Start managing the sound level around you and try to keep your deep work sessions at least 50 minutes long. If you need help blocking out background noise, use a white noise machine or soft background music. It makes you less likely to be disturbed by office chatter.
Right-size your equipment. Oversized monitors and cluttered desks can actually decrease efficiency. We often spend 15 percent more time searching for files when using a massive, unorganized desk compared to a compact, clean one. Also, we tend to get more distracted by notifications on large screens than on smaller, focused displays.
