TITLE: The Joy of Community Rescue
I serve with Neighborhood Paws, a local volunteer group in Green Valley dedicated to pet rescue and education. Attempting to save lost, injured, or abandoned pets can be truly painful; success is never guaranteed. However, when a rescue ends well, the feeling is simply wonderful.
I received a call from a resident in Northwood. she had discovered a very young kitten alone in a park. When I arrived, I saw a tiny creature, only about 3 weeks old. For safety, the woman had already put it in a small box.
I checked the kitten and it appeared healthy. If I could have found the mother cat, I would have reunited them, but I had no luck. My next task was to build a temporary shelter and secure it in a quiet corner of the park.
the homeowner was incredibly helpful. We found a small wooden crate. I lined it with soft towels and leaves to make the shelter safe and warm. Once I put the kitten inside, it stopped crying and soon became quiet.
Now we just needed the mother cat, but she was nowhere to be seen. I gave the resident a recording of the specific meows that kittens use when they are hungry. These sounds tell adult cats that a kitten is nearby; they might also lead our kitten to start calling for its mother. I shared as much advice as I could and went home to wait for any news during the night.
It was a restless night, to be sure, but sometimes nature has a way of helping us! The resident called to say the mother cat had heard the recording and returned. I drove back and found the kitten in the shelter, looking energetic. Best of all, it was eating a meal provided by its mother. The mother cat had returned to her role and would likely stay with her kitten.
