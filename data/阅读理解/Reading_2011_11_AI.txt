TITLE: Tool Use in Octopuses
For those who study the development of intelligence in the marine world, problem-solving is an important measurement. An animal that can use tools to solve a problem has a high level of intelligence.
Intelligence can be tested by studying whether the animal uses an object to achieve a goal, such as opening a container. Many marine animals fail this test, showing no interest in the objects around them. Only a few intelligent creatures like dolphins and octopuses have shown the ability to use tools in their environment.
Now another example has joined the list. In a recent scientific report, researchers found that a Veined Octopus has passed a complex tool-use test.
“We thought octopuses were the next key group to study,” said Dr. Miller of the Marine Institute, an author of the study. With their complex nervous systems, Dr. Miller said, octopuses “seem like ocean versions of primates.”
The researchers tested three octopuses—Inky, Blue, and Otto—at the City Aquarium. They placed a closed glass jar with food inside into the animals’ tank and recorded what happened with cameras.
The octopuses used their tentacles to feel the jar and to examine the lid.
Of the three, Inky then passed the test, in which a specific turning motion was required to open the jar. He could tell how to move the lid by trial and error, and he used his strength to unscrew the lid with his tentacles.
Dr. Miller said, “We knew octopuses were smart, but now we can describe their intelligence in a much better way.”
