TITLE: My Lifelong Connection with Community Tech Centers
I have always loved community tech centers. For as long as I can remember, technology was my passion. I was a focused learner, sometimes spending whole afternoons at the local center as a teenager. Coding was like a new world to me, and while other kids were playing sports, I was building small apps through the tools I used at the center.
My first part-time job was at the City Tech Lab when I was 17. It was a perfect start, and I did everything from organizing equipment to helping seniors learn how to use email.
As I grew older and started my own career, the tech center took on an added meaning in my life. I had two children, and these centers became our favorite place for learning. It was a highlight of our week to go to the neighborhood lab, where my kids could experiment with robots or work on digital art projects.
I always guided them, using different methods to make it fun, as if we were solving puzzles together. It was a special way to connect with my children and it gave them a sense of curiosity about the world.
Now, I see my children taking their own kids to such centers, and I love that this tradition of learning tech skills continues through the years.
As a software engineer, I have found a new way to relate to these centers. I encourage young developers to visit them when they need professional tools. I see these centers as a safe place for learners and creators, a bridge that connects a person with a dream. Tech centers help reduce the digital divide, and I believe all professionals should support them. Talk about them on social media and volunteer your time there when you can.
