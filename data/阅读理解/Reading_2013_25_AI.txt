TITLE: The Growth of the Independent Art Festival
In 1948, a group of famous figures from the art world, led by a Swiss conductor, decided to launch an international festival of music and theater in Zurich. The primary goal was to rebuild cultural links in Europe after the Second World War.
At the same time, the "Independent Showcase" emerged as a challenge to the official event. Six theater groups arrived without invitations in 1948, believing that every artist should have the right to perform. They held their shows in an old warehouse that had been empty for years.
Soon, groups of students, first from Zurich University and later from universities in Paris and London, began traveling to the city each summer. They performed plays by unknown writers in small community halls for the local people.
Today, the "Independent Showcase," which was once barely recognized, has grown far larger than the official festival, with about 1,200 performances of theater and dance during its 20-day run. Yet as early as 1960, when only 15 groups were involved, some critics already complained it was becoming too large.
The first professional manager was not hired until 1972. Today, there are ten full-time staff members, and this number jumps to over 140 during the peak month of August. In 2005, the event used 180 different locations to house over 1,600 shows by groups from 45 different countries. More than one million tickets were sold that year.
