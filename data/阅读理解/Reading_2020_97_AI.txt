TITLE: The Value of Returning to the Past
Revisiting a project you have worked on many times can feel like catching up with an old colleague. There is a sense of familiarity—but also a suspicion that time has changed you both, and thus your perspective. But the work doesn't change; people do. And that is what makes the act of revisiting so meaningful.
The beauty of looking back lies in the fact that our connection with the work depends on our current mindset. It is true that as I get older, I feel time moves faster. But with this process, it is all about the present. It is about what one brings to the moment, because revisiting is a conversation between the creator and the work.
There are three projects I revisit every year. The first, which I look at every spring, is a classic design from the 1980s. The style is truly impressive—a young designer looking back at a bold yet simpler era. Another is a poetic essay from 1995, a rambling piece about life’s small details. The third is a collection of old photos, because of the memories they hold.
While I often start new things, these three were given to me by mentors, which adds to the value I find in them. I believe that while money is important, giving your time to revisit an author’s work is the best way to honor them. The best projects are the ones that reveal more as time passes. But remember, it is you who must grow and revisit them in order to better understand your own history.
