TITLE: The Power of Community Volunteering
We have all seen it: in a local park, at a community center, or on a bus, surrounded by people who are focused on their own tasks or, worse, feeling completely alone despite being in a crowd.
What is the issue? It is possible that we have lost some of our social connection skills. It is more likely that few of us start a conversation with a stranger because it feels awkward, or we think it is not our business. But the next time you have a chance, consider that volunteering in your community is worth the effort. Experts say it is a great social activity that brings big benefits.
Thinking that small acts of helping are unimportant is a mistake. We must remember that strong communities wouldn't exist without these simple interactions. Volunteering is the "heart" of social life, says Dr. Mark Foster, a social researcher. "Almost every strong neighborhood bond begins with a small act of help," he explains. "The secret to successful volunteering is learning how to relate to others, not just finishing a task for them."
In a recent study, a professor invited people visiting a local library. One group was asked to have a short chat with the librarian; the other was asked to just return their books and leave. The results showed that those who talked with the staff reported much higher positive feelings and a better library experience. "It's not that talking to a librarian is better than talking to a friend," say the professor. "But these small interactions with people in our community matter for our happiness."
People who reach out to others feel a much stronger sense of belonging. Dr. Foster believes that building this sense of belonging starts with these simple, helpful actions. "Helping others is the basis of a healthy society," he says.
