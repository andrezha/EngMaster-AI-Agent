TITLE: The Sound of Success: Noise and Creativity
During a talk about my recent project, my host mentioned something I still think about. He said he prefers working in a busy campus cafe rather than his quiet private office. He told me, “The quiet makes it too hard to focus on new ideas.” His comment seemed odd to me. After all, cafes are full of noise. But I recently read a study that explains why he feels this way.
Researchers studied how different noise levels affect creative thinking. They divided people into four groups. Each group worked in a different environment: total silence, 50 decibels, 70 decibels, and 85 decibels. The results showed that the 70 decibels group—which is like the sound of a busy cafe—did much better than the others on creative tasks. There was little difference between the other groups.
This suggests that the right amount of background noise—neither too loud nor too quiet—can actually help people think more creatively. This level of sound might distract our minds just enough to let our imagination work, without making it impossible to concentrate. This state of “relaxed attention” seems to be perfect for creative work.
So why do some people dislike working in open-plan offices? The reason might be that in an office, you often hear specific conversations that you can't help listening to. Researchers found that while general background noise helps, hearing actual conversations can be distracting. A cafe or a shared workspace provides noise without these personal interruptions.
