TITLE: The Visionary Designer
While world-renowned designers are often picked to create landmark structures in emerging cities, many talented local architects are striving to gain international recognition.
Their hard work has paid off. Li Wei, a 45-year-old local designer, received the Global Urban Excellence Award—often called the "Oscar of Urban Design"—last March. He is the first person from his region to earn this distinction.
Li serves as the lead designer at the Institute of Creative Spaces (ICS). His main workshop is located at the South Hill campus of a technical university. Many of the structures on that campus were his personal projects.
The aesthetic of the campus is strikingly different from typical modern buildings. Visitors are frequently surprised by the varied use of space and the mix of construction materials. The shapes of the halls perfectly follow the natural curves of the hills, creating a balanced landscape.
Li collected over 5 million pieces of discarded stone from different decades. He directed craftsmen to use ancient methods to turn the stones into walls, paths, and bridges. This work drew massive interest because it combined modern functionality with traditional local styles.
Li’s projects demonstrate a deep grasp of modern needs and a respect for historical heritage. Through this balance, he has defined a new direction for local design, according to a previous award winner.
Li believes that heritage should not be kept only in history books. “That just shows that it used to exist,” he said.
“Many people misunderstand what heritage is. They think it refers only to old items. In reality, it includes things that are still evolving and being created today,” he added.
“Currently, many are copying global trends instead of looking at their own roots. People often discuss heritage without truly knowing what it involves,” said Li.
The study of such roots must be linked with real projects. Otherwise, the result will feel fake and empty.
