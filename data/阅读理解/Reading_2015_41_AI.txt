TITLE: A Weekend at the Innovation Fair
The gray, rainy winter in London had not been an ideal environment for my internship, so when the opportunity arrived for a short trip to Austin, Texas, I was ready to go in an instant. I headed for the city of energy and bright skies, dreaming of outdoor events and green parks. When our plane arrived to see clear sunshine and warm air, I felt a deep sense of relief. Weekend activities like kayaking, local music, and late-night talks (at reasonable hours, not in the middle of a workday) were great, but the highlight — especially for my mind, tired from months of repetitive office tasks — was a 7 a.m. trip to the Austin Makers’ Fair that turned out to be worth every bit of lost sleep.
The fair, established in 1985, happens every Saturday from 7 a.m. to 2 p.m. in the downtown square. There were tables of clever wooden crafts, the bright blue signs of the local tech start-up trucks, and most of all, the hand-built robots: small, flexible, and beautifully designed machines.
Disappointed by many low-quality gadgets I’d bought online, I had stopped purchasing new tech tools for a long time. No matter how sleek they looked in advertisements, once I tested them at home, they were always slow, fragile, and useless. But I walked, with some doubt, toward one particular stand at the "Green Circuit" corner, which was filled with miniature drones the size of my palm. These were high-quality products — and right then, I understood that the best part of being in Austin was finding things that back in my London office I wouldn’t see for another year.
Though I was impressed by the drones, my mood improved further when I found out that the Green Circuit team also provides parts for "The Hub", a newly launched tech space at the local university where — fortunately — I was invited to visit that evening. Even before looking at their project list, I knew I would be interested in everything they had.
