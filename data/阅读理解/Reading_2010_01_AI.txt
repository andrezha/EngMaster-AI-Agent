TITLE: The Campus Green Garden Project
RIVERVIEW --- Every morning at West Hills High School, teachers and staff flock to a unique project: the school’s first organic garden run mostly by students with special learning needs.
Well before the first bell rings, students pick fresh vegetables and fruits. Then, during the first period, staff members send their orders through a mobile app, and students prepare the delivery bags.
By the time the garden gate closes at 10:00 a.m., the project usually sells out of its daily stock. “Dr. Miller said the organic tomatoes we grew were the best she’s ever tasted,” Sarah Johnson, a junior student, announced recently after receiving an app notification from the teacher.
The project is called Green Future, which aims to provide real-world experience. Although some of the students do not have disabilities, many do, and the project helps them prepare for life after graduation.
They learn not only how to grow organic plants but also how to manage business tasks. They keep a work log and receive small rewards, which they record in their personal accounts.
Special-education teachers Mr. Black and Ms. White introduced Green Future from a similar program in another city. It was not easy. Mr. Black’s first challenge was related to safety. Should students use sharp tools? What about organic standards?
Local food expert Dr. Miller helped. She made sure all the farming methods, which avoid chemical use, fell within healthy guidelines.
The whole school has joined in to help. Teachers agreed to share part of their outdoor space. Art students designed the garden signs on the fence. Business students helped create the reward system. The school sports team helped pay for the watering tools.
